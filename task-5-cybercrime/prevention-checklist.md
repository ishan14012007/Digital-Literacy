# Stay Safe Online: A Survival Checklist
Being a student in India right now means you're basically living on your phone—from attendance to food delivery. Here is a straightforward list of how to keep your data and your money where they belong:

Treat Your UPI PIN Like an ATM PIN: It sounds obvious, but never share it. Most importantly, remember the golden rule: You only enter your PIN to send money, never to receive it. If someone sends you a QR code saying "scan this to receive your cashback/refund," it is a scam 100% of the time.

Lock Down Your Banking Apps: Don't just rely on your phone's main passcode. Enable the internal app lock feature for apps like GPay, PhonePe, or BHIM. If someone borrows your phone to "make a quick call," they shouldn't be able to open your wallet.

Use a Separate "Junk" Email: Create a secondary email address for signing up for random student discounts, campus Wi-Fi, or online competitions. This keeps your primary Gmail (linked to your bank and college ID) out of the hands of data brokers and hackers.

Watch Out for "Part-Time Job" Texts: If you get a WhatsApp message offering ₹5,000 a day for "liking YouTube videos" or "rating hotels," block them immediately. These are "Task Frauds" designed to lure students into a trap where you eventually have to pay "fees" to get your earnings out.

Turn on Two-Factor Authentication (2FA): Use an authenticator app (like Google Authenticator) for your Instagram and LinkedIn. If a hacker gets your password through a data breach, they still won't be able to get into your account without that secondary code on your physical phone.

Avoid Charging at Public USB Stations: In places like airports or major railway stations, "juice jacking" is a real thing. Hackers can modify USB ports to install malware on your phone while it charges. Use your own adapter and plug into a standard wall socket instead.

Verify "Official" Callers: If you get a call from someone claiming to be from "the police," "Aadhaar office," or "your bank" saying your account is blocked, hang up. Call the official customer care number found on the back of your card or the official website to verify. No government official will ever ask for an OTP over the phone.

Audit Your Permissions: Every few weeks, go into your settings and see which apps have access to your "Contacts" and "Location." If a basic calculator or wallpaper app is asking for your contact list, delete it—it’s likely harvesting your data.

If You Get Scammed, Act Fast:

The first hour is the most critical for recovering lost money. Don't be embarrassed—report it immediately.

Portal: https://www.cybercrime.gov.in

Helpline: 1930 (Keep your transaction ID and bank details ready when you call).
